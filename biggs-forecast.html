Calibration

If you are going to weight any of this in a financial decision, these are the facts about the source that matter most. All of them come from his own mouth, not from critics.

"Sometimes it comes really quick and sometimes he's showing me years in advance and I don't know."

He describes his own timing as a depth-perception problem and gives a worked example: in 2018 he was shown chickens becoming expensive and eggs hard to get, bought twenty chickens on it, watched three years pass with nothing, apologised to his wife for missing it, and then saw egg prices spike in 2023. Using this for an economic decision

Three of these claims are falsifiable on a normal investing horizon, and they are the ones to score first, because they tell you what the rest of the ledger is worth: silver reaching $75 an ounce, gold clearing $10,000, and the tariff-contingent crash he tied to a Supreme Court ruling in November 2025. Each has a number, an instrument and a window. Check them against the market yourself rather than against a later video, since a forecast re-told after the fact tends to acquire the outcome.

Note also where the forecasts and the instructions come apart. His instruction set — no debt, cash and food buffer, a fuel-efficient car, medication on hand, a garden — is roughly what a conventional adviser would call resilience, and it costs you little if none of the visions land. The asset calls are the opposite: they only pay if the specific vision is right and the timing is close, and his own worked example puts the timing error at five years.
